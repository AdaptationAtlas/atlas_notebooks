details:
  title: "How to interpret precipitation agreement"
  body: |-
    **Agreement score (0–4)** — Counts how many of the four precipitation products meet the consistency criterion for an area. A score of 4 means all four agree on the broad pattern of precipitation variability; 0 means none meet the criterion.

    **What agreement means** — Higher agreement gives more confidence that the pattern is not unique to one product. It does not prove that the estimated rainfall amount is correct: products can agree while sharing biases, especially where ground observations are sparse.

Reliable climate information services also depend on consistent satellite data products. This section shows how different satellite rainfall products capture similar patterns of climate variability, which is an indication of trustworthy, well-calibrated observational models. Here, we evaluate the alignment of satellite climate datasets align with each other in Africa. Consistency across data sources reflects observation density and effectiveness of satellite retrieval algorithms. Data sources evaluated: CHIRPS, AgERA5, IMERG, and MSWEP. The consistency level ranges from 0 (no agreement) to 4 (agreement across all evaluated data sources).
